What Garage Door Gear Repair Wilmington DE Involves

Garage Door Gear Repair Wilmington DE is the specialized fix for the nylon or steel gears and the drive sprocket inside your automatic opener. Our Gear and Sprocket Repair Process

Every job follows the same five steps, whether you have a Craftsman chain drive in The Highlands or a belt-drive LiftMaster in a Riverfront townhome. Here is exactly what happens from the moment you call.

STEP 01

Phone Diagnosis

You describe the noise, the door behavior, and the opener brand. We ask a few quick questions to confirm it is a gear or sprocket issue and not a stripped chain, dead motor, or misaligned sensor. Most calls take five minutes, and we give you a price range before we ever roll a truck.

Tools and methods

Tools and Methods We Use

01

Gear puller and press

Removes the worn gear from the opener shaft without bending the shaft or cracking the housing. A hammer and screwdriver will damage the shaft, which is why we use the right puller every time.

02

Socket wrench set and vise grips

Used to unbolt the opener from the rail, remove the housing cover, and hold the shaft steady during the gear swap. Standard and metric sizes cover every major opener brand.

03

Winding bars and C-clamps

When the repair requires spring work to fix door balance, we use winding bars to safely release and re-tension torsion springs. C-clamps hold the door in place while we work.

04

Tape measure and level

We check rail alignment, chain sag, and door level after every repair. A door that is out of level drags on one side and wears the gear unevenly.

05

Rubber mallet and screwdriver set

Used for seating the new gear, tapping the housing back into place, and adjusting limit screws. A rubber mallet will not dent the housing or crack the plastic cover.

06

Safety glasses and gloves

Old grease, metal shavings, and spring tension are all hazards. We wear eye and hand protection on every job, and we keep the work area clear while the opener is open.

07

Lithium grease and cleaner

We scrape out old grease and debris, then pack the housing with fresh lithium grease. This is what keeps the new gear running quiet for years instead of months.

08

UL 325 safety reverse testing

After every gear repair, we test the safety reverse with a 2x4 under the door and confirm it reverses within two seconds of contact. This is the same standard the opener was built to, and it is not optional on our jobs.

09

DASMA door balance check

We disconnect the opener and lift the door halfway by hand. If it will not hold position, the springs are out of balance and the gear will strip again. We fix the balance before we call the gear repair done.

10

Manufacturer chain tension spec

Every opener brand publishes a chain tension spec, usually about half an inch of play at the rail midpoint. We set tension to that number, not by feel, because too tight strips gears and too loose slaps the rail.

11

Force and travel limit calibration

After the gear swap, we re-set the open and close travel limits and dial in the force setting. This prevents the opener from straining against the stops or reversing on its own mid-travel.

12

Root cause inspection

A stripped gear is a symptom, not the disease. We check door weight, spring tension, roller condition, and chain tension on every job so the new gear is not set up to fail the same way.

Problems we solve

Gear Problems We Fix Every Week

Gear and sprocket failures show up in a handful of predictable ways. If any of these sound familiar, the gear is the likely culprit and we can confirm it on-site.

01

Opener hums but the door does not move

We pull the opener housing, confirm the gear is stripped, and install a new gear and sprocket kit. Most jobs finish in under two hours, and we test the door through 10 full cycles before we leave. The motor runs, the gear spins in place, and the door stays put. Every cycle grinds more plastic off the gear and fills the housing with debris, which can seize the motor if you keep hitting the button.

02

Loud grinding noise every time the door moves

We replace the gear set, clean the housing, and re-grease. We also check chain tension and door balance, because a tight chain or a heavy door is usually what caused the wear in the first place. That grinding is gear teeth shearing off one by one. Left alone, the opener will eventually lock up mid-travel with the door stuck open or closed, which is a security and safety problem.

03

Door moves a few inches then stops

We inspect the gear, sprocket, and chain together. If the gear is the failure point, we replace it and reset the travel limits. If the motor is also damaged, we tell you straight and quote both options. A partially stripped gear catches on the remaining teeth and stalls. The opener's circuit board may also trip on overload, and repeated stalls can burn out the motor or the capacitor.

04

Opener works fine but the door is very heavy to lift by hand

We test door balance by disconnecting the opener and lifting the door halfway. If it will not hold, we address the torsion springs or rollers first, then repair the gear so the fix actually lasts. A heavy door overloads the gear on every cycle. Even a brand-new gear will strip again in months if the underlying balance problem is not fixed at the same time.
