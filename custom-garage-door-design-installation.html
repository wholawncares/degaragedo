How service works

How Our Custom Door Process Works

Five steps from first call to final cycle test. No mystery, no surprise line items, and no waiting three weeks for a callback.

STEP 01

We come out, measure the opening with a tape measure and laser, check the header for level, and look at the existing track and springs. You get a written spec sheet the same visit. Tools and methods

Tools and Methods We Use

01

Winding Bars

Used to safely tension and release torsion springs. A torsion spring stores enough energy to cause serious injury, so we never touch one without the right bars and a firm stance.

02

Socket Wrench Set and Power Drill

For bolting track brackets, hinges, and end plates, and for driving lag bolts into the header. We torque to spec so nothing works loose after a thousand cycles.

03

Tape Measure and Level

We check the opening width, height, and header level before we order. A 1/4-inch sag in the header changes the door spec, and we catch it on the measure, not on install day.

04

C-Clamps and Vise Grips

Used to hold track and panels in place while we set brackets and align the door. Keeps the door square while we work and prevents panels from shifting mid-install.

05

Rubber Mallet and Screwdriver Set

For seating panels, adjusting hinges, and setting rollers without denting the door face. Small tools, but they are the difference between a clean install and a scratched one.

06

Safety Glasses

Worn on every install. Springs, cables, and metal shavings are all eye hazards, and we do not cut corners on PPE.

07

UL 325 Safety Compliance

Every opener we install or service is set up to UL 325, which covers entrapment protection. That means the safety reverse is tested on contact and the photo-eye sensors are aligned and functional before we leave. It is the standard that keeps kids and pets safe around a moving door.

08

ANSI/DASMA 102 Installation Standard

We install to ANSI/DASMA 102, the industry standard for garage door and opener installation. It covers clearances, fastening, spring sizing, and track alignment. Following it is why our doors run quiet and stay level for years.

09

Torsion Spring Weight Matching

We weigh the door or use the manufacturer's spec to select the correct torsion spring wire gauge and length. A spring sized wrong for the door is the fastest way to burn out an opener or snap a cable. We do the math before we hang anything.

10

NEC-Compliant Wiring

When we wire an opener or add a receptacle in the garage, we follow NEC electrical code. That means proper gauge wire, correct breaker sizing, and grounded circuits. It matters when an inspector or a buyer's home inspector looks at the work.

Problems we solve

Problems a Custom Door Solves for Wilmington Homes

Most people call us because something is failing, not because they woke up wanting a new door. Here are the problems a custom install actually fixes.

01

Door is dented, rusted, or falling apart

We replace the door with a custom panel built to your opening, install new springs and cables sized to the weight, and haul the old door away. You get a door that seals, runs quiet, and holds its finish. A failing door drags on the opener, strains the springs, and looks bad from the street. It also lets water and cold air into the garage, which you feel in the room above it.

02

Odd opening size no stock door fits

We measure your actual opening and build to it. Whether it is an 8-foot single or a 17-foot two-car with a low header, the door is made for that space. No shims, no gaps, no compromise. A stock door on a non-standard opening leaves gaps at the sides and bottom. You get drafts, water intrusion, and a door that rattles every time the opener runs.

03

Garage is freezing in winter

We install an insulated custom door with a polyurethane or polystyrene core and a tight bottom seal. That keeps the garage warmer and cuts the load on your HVAC. Most homeowners notice the difference the first cold snap. An uninsulated door on an attached garage pulls heat out of the house all winter. Your furnace runs longer, your energy bill climbs, and the room above the garage stays cold.

04

Noisy door wakes the house

We hang nylon rollers, new track, and properly sized torsion springs, and pair it with a belt-drive LiftMaster or Chamberlain opener. The door goes from a grind to a hum you barely hear from inside. Worn steel rollers and a tired torsion spring make a racket every time the door moves. If a bedroom sits over the garage, that 6 a.m. departure wakes everyone up.
