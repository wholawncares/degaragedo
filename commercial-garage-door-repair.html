Tools and methods

Tools and Methods We Use

01

Winding Bars

Used to safely release and re-tension torsion springs. A commercial torsion spring stores enough energy to cause serious injury, so winding bars and proper procedure are non-negotiable on every spring job.

02

Vise Grips and C-Clamps

Used to lock the door and the torsion shaft in place before we release tension. They keep the door from moving while we work, which is the difference between a safe repair and a dangerous one.

03

Socket Wrench Set and Power Drill

Used to remove and re-bolt track brackets, hinges, drums and operator mounts. Commercial hardware is heavier than residential, so we carry the sizes that fit commercial doors and operators.

04

Tape Measure and Level

Used to align vertical and horizontal track, set bracket heights, and confirm the door hangs square. A track that is off by a quarter inch will wear rollers and cables fast.

05

Rubber Mallet and Screwdriver Set

Used for panel adjustments, hinge work and sensor alignment. A rubber mallet lets us seat panels and brackets without denting the steel.

06

Safety Glasses

Worn on every job. Springs, cables and metal shavings are the obvious hazards, and we do not work without eye protection. It is a small thing that separates a professional crew from a careless one.

07

UL 325 Safety Standard Testing

UL 325 covers the safety requirements for commercial door operators, including reversing systems and entrapment protection. We test every photo eye, reversing edge and operator stop before we leave. That keeps your door compliant and your crew safe.

08

ANSI/DASMA 102 Guidelines

ANSI/DASMA 102 sets the standard for door and operator installation. We follow it on spring sizing, track alignment, bracket placement and operator setup. That is how a repair holds up under commercial cycle counts instead of failing in a year.

09

DASMA-Certified Workmanship

Our technicians hold DASMA certification for door and operator work. That means the repair is done to industry standard, not to whatever was fastest. For property managers and facility directors, it is documentation you can point to if an inspector asks.

10

OSHA Safety Compliance

Overhead doors fall under OSHA safety requirements for general industry. We work to those standards on every job, from lockout of power before we start to testing the door after the repair. It protects your crew and ours.

Problems we solve

Commercial Door Problems We Fix in Wilmington

Most commercial door calls come down to a handful of failures. Here are the ones we see most in Wilmington, what happens if you ignore them, and how we fix them.

01

Broken torsion spring leaves the door too heavy to lift

We replace the torsion spring with one sized to the door weight and cycle life, using winding bars and proper safety procedure. Then we check the operator for strain damage. Standard spring replacement runs about 90 minutes, and we stock common sizes on the van. The operator strains and burns out trying to lift a door it was never sized to move alone. You lose the door and the operator, doubling the repair bill.

02

Snapped cable drops the door crooked or off track

We isolate power, secure the door, and replace the galvanized steel cable. We inspect the drums for wear, re-spool the cable, and re-tension the springs. Cable replacement usually runs 60 to 90 minutes, and we check the other cable while we are there. A door hanging on one cable is dangerous and can come down hard. Forcing it risks injury and bends the track, turning a cable job into a track and panel replacement.

03

Door off track and jammed in the opening

We isolate power, secure the door with clamps, and re-set it on the track. Then we find the cause: worn rollers, bent track, loose brackets or a failed hinge. Off-track repair usually takes one to two hours, and we replace what caused it so it does not happen again. A jammed door blocks your bay or dock, stops deliveries, and can damage panels and track if forced. It is also a safety hazard for anyone working near it.

04

Opener motor failure or stripped gears

We diagnose the operator, check the gears, sprockets, chain and logic board, and repair or replace what failed. For stripped gears, we replace the gear and sprocket set and re-tension the chain. Most operator repairs finish in under two hours. A dead operator means the door will not move at all, even if the door itself is fine. If the gears are stripped, the motor hums but nothing happens, and the door stays stuck.
