Service overview

What Garage Door Sensor Repair Wilmington DE Involves

Garage Door Sensor Repair Wilmington DE is the diagnostic and repair work that restores the two photo-eye sensors mounted at the bottom of your door tracks. Each unit shoots an invisible infrared beam across the opening; if the beam breaks or the signal drops, the opener reverses or refuses to close. We test voltage at the head unit, check the sending and receiving eyes with a meter, inspect the low-voltage wiring for corrosion or rodent damage, and confirm the lenses are clean and aimed at each other. Most sensor faults trace back to misalignment, a dead eye, or a loose wire staple.

In Wilmington, sensor problems show up year-round. Winter road salt kicks up onto the lenses, summer humidity fogs the housings, and older doors in neighborhoods like The Highlands and Wawaset Park often still run original sensors from the 1990s. If your garage door sensor is not working in Wilmington DE, we can usually tell you why within the first 15 minutes on site. We stock LiftMaster, Chamberlain, Genie, and Linear replacement eyes on the truck, so most sensor repair Wilmington Delaware calls finish in a single visit.

Tools and methods

Tools and Methods We Use

01

Digital Multimeter

Tests voltage at the opener head and at each sensor to isolate whether the fault is the eye, the wire, or the board.

02

Tone Generator and Probe

Traces low-voltage sensor wire through walls and along the header when the run is buried or stapled out of sight.

03

Socket Wrench Set and Screwdriver Set

Removes and remounts sensor brackets, track hardware, and opener terminal covers without stripping fasteners.

04

Torpedo Level and Tape Measure

Sets both eyes to the same height, 4 to 6 inches off the floor, and confirms the brackets are square to the door opening.

05

LiftMaster and Chamberlain Sensor Kits

Brand-matched replacement eyes and brackets carried on every truck for the two most common opener brands in Wilmington.

06

Genie, Linear, and Marantec Sensor Kits

Covers older and less common openers so we can finish in one visit instead of ordering parts.

07

Safety Glasses and Vise Grips

Standard PPE and a way to hold brackets steady during alignment so the aim does not drift while tightening.

08

UL 325 Reverse-on-Obstruction Test

We lay a 2x4 flat in the door's path and confirm the opener reverses within 2 seconds of contact. This is the same test a home inspector runs, and it is the clearest proof the safety circuit is working.

09

Photo-Eye Height Verification

UL 325 requires the beam to sit no more than 6 inches off the floor. We measure both eyes with a tape and adjust the brackets so the beam catches pets, toys, and small children, not just adults.

10

Low-Voltage Circuit Trace

We meter the circuit from the opener terminal to each sensor, checking for voltage drop that points to a corroded splice or a pinched wire. This is how we find the faults that a visual inspection misses.

11

DASMA-Aligned Bracket Mounting

We mount sensor brackets per DASMA guidance, using rigid hardware and keeping the run clear of the door's travel path. That stops the vibration that causes repeat misalignment.

12

NEC-Compliant Wire Routing

Low-voltage sensor wire is routed and stapled per NEC rules, away from line-voltage runs, so the signal stays clean and the install passes inspection.

Problems we solve

Sensor Problems We Fix Every Week

Most sensor calls fall into a handful of patterns. Here is what we see most often in Wilmington and how we handle each one.

01

Door reverses for no reason mid-close

We meter both eyes, check for a broken beam, and inspect the wiring for a loose staple or pinch. Most cases trace to a misaligned eye or a failing receiving unit. We realign or replace, then verify reverse-on-obstruction with a 2x4. Leaves the house open to weather and theft, and can crush anything in the door's path if the safety circuit is bypassed.

02

Sensor LED blinking instead of steady

Blinking usually means misalignment or a dead eye. We test each unit with a meter, swap the failed one for a brand-matched replacement, and confirm both LEDs go steady before we leave. A blinking LED means the opener is not receiving a clean signal, so the door will not close from the remote or wall button.

03

One sensor dark, no LED at all

We trace the low-voltage run from the opener head, test for voltage at the sensor, and isolate whether the fault is the wire, the eye, or the board. Then we repair the actual failed component instead of guessing. A dark eye means no power is reaching the unit, which points to a wiring fault or a failed opener circuit board.

04

Sensors knocked out of alignment by a bumped door

We re-level both brackets, tighten the mounting hardware, and check the track for bends that caused the shift. If the bracket itself is bent, we replace it with a rigid unit so it holds the aim. Even a small bump can shift a bracket a quarter inch, which is enough to break the beam and stop the door from closing.
