Tools and methods

Tools and Methods We Use

01

Winding Bars and C-Clamps

Used to safely release and re-tension torsion springs. Winding bars hold the tension while C-clamps lock the torsion bar in place. This is the part of the job that hurts people who try it without the right tools.

02

Socket Wrench Set and Power Drill

For removing and re-fitting brackets, hinges, and track bolts. We carry both standard and metric sockets to match the hardware on LiftMaster, Chamberlain, Genie, and Craftsman openers.

03

Tape Measure and Level

Used to check track plumb, door square, and spring wind counts. A door that is a quarter inch out of level will wear rollers and cables fast, so we measure before we tighten anything.

04

Rubber Mallet and Vise Grips

For seating rollers, adjusting hinges, and holding parts during a repair. The mallet lets us nudge panels and track without denting the steel.

05

Safety Glasses and Gloves

Non-negotiable on every spring and cable job. Torsion springs store enough energy to cause serious injury, and we treat every job with that in mind.

06

UL 325 Safety Standard

Every opener we touch gets tested against UL 325, which covers the auto-reverse and entrapment protection. We check the photo-eye sensors, the force settings, and the manual release. A repair that skips this test is not finished.

07

DASMA and IDEA Standards

We follow DASMA guidance on spring cycle ratings and door balance, and we work to IDEA standards for installation and service. That means the parts we install are rated for the door weight, not just whatever fits.

08

NEC Electrical Code

Opener wiring, outlets, and low-voltage sensor runs are checked against NEC code. We do not leave a job with a spliced extension cord powering an opener, no matter how late it is.

09

OSHA Safety Compliance

Our techs work to OSHA safety standards on every job. That covers ladder use, lockout of the torsion system, and keeping the work area clear so nobody in your household walks into a hazard.

Where this service fits

When to Call for Emergency Service

01

A spring snaps at 6 a.m. on a workday and the car is inside the garage.

We prioritize morning spring calls so you can get to work. A tech arrives, swaps both springs, re-balances the door, and tests the opener. Most jobs finish inside 90 minutes on site. You are on the road within two hours of the call, with a door that runs quiet and level.

02

A storm knocks the door off track and it is hanging crooked in the opening.

We secure the door first so it cannot fall, then reset the rollers, straighten or replace the bent track section, and re-level the verticals. We check the cables and drums while we are in there. The door closes and locks the same night, and the house is sealed against the weather.

03

The opener dies on a Sunday evening and the door will not budge.

We come out, confirm whether it is the motor, the logic board, or the safety sensors, and repair or replace the unit. We program your remotes and keypad and test the auto-reverse. You get a working door before Monday morning, with the opener set to code.

04

A cable snaps and the door drops crooked, jamming one side.

We replace both galvanized steel cables as a pair, inspect the drums for grooves or cracks, and re-balance the door. Running a door on one cable is never safe. The door travels level on both sides, and the drums and cables are matched for the long haul.
